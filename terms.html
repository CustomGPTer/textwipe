Using TextWipe within the law

TextWipe is for a phone you own. Putting it on someone else's phone, using it to follow where someone goes, or wiping a phone to get rid of evidence can be a crime.

  • Computer Misuse Act 1990. Installing or using TextWipe on a phone that is not yours, without the owner's permission, to get at it or to lock, change or erase it, can be an offence under sections 1 and 3.
  • Protection from Harassment Act 1997. Using LOCATE to keep track of where another person goes can be stalking under section 2A.
  • Destroying evidence. Wiping or resetting a phone to stop the police or a court seeing what is on it can be perverting the course of justice, a serious offence under the common law of England and Wales.

Laws differ in other countries. If you are not in the UK, the rules where you are apply, and you are responsible for following them. Before downloading from this site you are asked to confirm: “I understand and will use this app within the law”.

Wiping and resetting

A wipe or factory reset cannot be undone. TextWipe warns for 15 seconds first and your PIN can cancel it, but once it starts, files that have been overwritten are gone. Overwriting a file once is a best effort on flash storage and cannot promise forensic erasure, and copies in the cloud or other apps are not touched. Keep your PIN private and your Google account details safe.

What Android decides

Android can delay or block texts, alarms and background work, for example after Force stop, with battery saving, or before the first unlock after a restart. Android 14 and later only allows a full reset when TextWipe is the phone's Device Owner.
